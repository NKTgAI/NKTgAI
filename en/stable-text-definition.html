STABLE Text Definition

STABLE Text is text whose semantic energy is evenly balanced between AMP (Amplifying) and DAMP (Damping), so that no component clearly dominates the logical structure of the whole text. In STABLE Text, information is mainly organized around objective description, conveying data, or technical explanation, rather than emphasizing execution or condition analysis.

Characteristics of STABLE Text

  • Semantic energy is relatively evenly distributed across sentences.
  • No single Core Content stands out.
  • Sentences carry roughly equivalent informational value.
  • The text leans toward conveying knowledge rather than driving action.

Identification Criteria

  • AMP ≈ DAMP (a balanced energy state on NKTg AI's scale).
  • Semantic energy does not converge strongly on action sentences (AMP), nor is it mostly scattered across analytical sentences (DAMP).
  • The system determines: No Dominant Core Content exists.

Example

Text:

"The office space is designed with an emphasis on natural light flow and air circulation. Functional areas are separated by patches of greenery rather than rigid partitions. Soft light spreads evenly across the space, creating a relaxing feeling for people at work. The interior focuses on minimalism, with neutral colors and nature-friendly materials. Quietness is a defining feature of this environment, where the sound of nature outside connects harmoniously with the interior through open design."

NKTg AI determines:

  • AMP ≈ 50%
  • DAMP ≈ 50%
  • Core Content: Not prominent.

This is STABLE Text, because the content only conveys an objective description of the environment, gives no orders, assigns no tasks, and has no sentence that dominates the whole text.

Short Definition

STABLE Text is text whose semantic energy is balanced between AMP and DAMP, where information is presented objectively and evenly, with no prominent Core Content dominating the overall logical structure of the text.
